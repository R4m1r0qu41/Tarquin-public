# Código Civil Federal (MX) art. 1840 — Cláusula penal (pena convencional)

## Summary

Art. 1840 lets contracting parties stipulate a *prestación* as a **pena** for non-performance
or non-conforming performance; where they do, **damages (daños y perjuicios) may not also be
claimed** — the pena substitutes for, and is exclusive of, actual-loss recovery. The
surrounding articles complete the rule: art. 1842 — the creditor need not prove harm and the
debtor cannot escape by proving none; **art. 1843 — the cláusula penal may not exceed, in
value or amount, the principal obligation**; art. 1844 — partial performance reduces the pena
proportionally.

## Why it matters here

This is the Mexican analog to the UCC liquidated-damages frame and closes the long-standing
`concept-liquidated-damages` MX gap. Two contrasts matter for any cross-jurisdiction artifact:
the MX pena is **exclusive** of actual damages (unlike a UCC liquidated-damages clause, which
interacts with other remedies) and is subject to a **hard statutory cap at the principal**
(art. 1843), rather than the UCC's reasonableness standard. An agreement's enforceability
analysis must not assume the US frame applies in a Mexican-law contract.
