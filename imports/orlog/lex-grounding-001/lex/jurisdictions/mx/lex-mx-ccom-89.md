# Código de Comercio (MX) art. 89 — Comercio electrónico: principios y definiciones

## Summary

Art. 89 opens Título II ("Del Comercio Electrónico"). It states the rules govern commercial
matters throughout Mexico and subjects their interpretation to the principles of
**neutralidad tecnológica, autonomía de la voluntad, compatibilidad internacional**, and
**equivalencia funcional** — functional equivalence of a *Mensaje de Datos* to information
recorded on non-electronic media, and of a *Firma Electrónica* to a handwritten (autograph)
signature. It authorises electronic, optical, or other technological means in commercial
acts and defines the operative terms, including *Certificado*, *Datos de Creación de Firma
Electrónica*, *Mensaje de Datos*, *Firma Electrónica*, and *Firma Electrónica Avanzada o
Fiable* (one meeting the art. 97 fracc. I–IV requirements). Art. 89 bis bars denial of legal
effect, validity, or binding force to any act solely because it is expressed as a Mensaje de
Datos.

## Why it matters here

This is the Mexican commercial-law anchor for the functional equivalence of an electronic
signature to an autograph signature — the legal basis any artifact or attestation must rest
on before treating a Mexican electronically-signed commercial document as effective. It is
the MX counterpart to the US e-signature nodes and the grounding a future verification
attestation would cite for `document_authenticity` / `signer_identity` findings.
