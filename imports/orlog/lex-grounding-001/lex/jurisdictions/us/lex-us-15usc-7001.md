# 15 U.S.C. § 7001 — Electronic records and signatures in commerce (ESIGN)

## Summary

§ 7001(a) provides that, with respect to any transaction in or affecting interstate or foreign
commerce, a signature, contract, or other record relating to the transaction may not be denied
legal effect, validity, or enforceability **solely because it is in electronic form**, and a
contract may not be denied legal effect **solely because an electronic signature or electronic
record was used in its formation**. Later subsections preserve consumer-disclosure protections
and carve out certain documents.

## Why it matters here

This is the federal baseline for treating an electronic signature/record as legally effective
in US commerce. It is the US counterpart to the Mexican functional-equivalence rule and the
floor above which New York's ESRA operates. A verification attestation about a US
electronically-signed document should cite ESIGN plus the controlling state rule, never ESIGN
alone, because state law (here ESRA, not UETA) shapes the actual effect.
