# 15 U.S.C. § 77b — Securities Act of 1933 § 2: Definitions

## Summary

Definitional backbone of the Securities Act. § 77b(a)(1) defines **security** broadly —
notes, stocks, bonds, evidences of indebtedness, investment contracts, options, and any
interest "commonly known as a 'security'". § 77b(a)(3) defines **sale**/**offer**: every
contract of sale or disposition of a security for value, and every attempt or offer to
dispose of (or solicitation of an offer to buy) a security for value; preliminary
negotiations between an issuer and underwriter are excluded.

## Why it matters here

A fixed-interest promissory note is a "note" and therefore presumptively within
§ 77b(a)(1); whether a particular commercial note is a "security" is a legal judgment
(e.g. family-resemblance analysis) that Orlog artifacts must NOT decide —
artifacts only carry the parties' representation about it. Every securities SLC's notion
of "offer" and "sale" resolves here.
