# New York CPLR § 4539 — Reproductions of original

## Summary

§ 4539(b) provides that a reproduction created by a process that stores an image of a writing
and **does not permit additions, deletions, or changes without leaving a record** of them is,
**when authenticated by competent testimony or affidavit** that includes the manner or method
by which tampering or degradation is prevented, **as admissible in evidence as the original**.
Subdivision (a) covers reproductions made in the regular course of business.

## Why it matters here

This is the New York evidentiary hook for tamper-evident electronic reproductions — the rule
that lets a digitally preserved document stand in for the original in court, conditioned on
authenticating proof of the anti-tampering method. It maps directly to a verification engine's
integrity/evidentiary-weight outputs, and it draws the line the epistemic discipline insists
on: **admissible as a reproduction ≠ authentic ≠ legally effective**. Its tamper-evidence
predicate is exactly what a cryptographic integrity finding can help establish (but does not by
itself satisfy).
