# New York State Technology Law § 304 — Use of electronic signatures (ESRA)

## Summary

§ 304(2) provides that, unless specifically provided otherwise by law, an electronic signature
may be used by a person **in lieu of a signature affixed by hand**, and that the use of an
electronic signature **shall have the same validity and effect** as a hand-affixed signature.
§ 304(1) tasks the electronic facilitator with rules governing the use of electronic
signatures and authentication, while barring rules that apportion fault or limit liability for
their use. ESRA (State Technology Law art. 3, §§ 301–309) carries exemptions for certain
instruments (e.g., specified testamentary and health-care documents).

## Why it matters here

This is the New York state electronic-signature rule — the controlling regime for NY because
New York did not adopt UETA. It pins the equivalence of an electronic signature to a
hand-written one in New York and is the state-law half (with [[lex-us-15usc-7001]]) of any
finding that a NY electronically-signed document is effective.
